Anthropogenic Disturbance to Beluga Whale Pods During Hudson Bay Migration

Arctic Field Biology Course, University of Toronto Mississauga & University of Manitoba · Churchill, MB · July – August 2023 · Supervisors: Peter Kotanen, Stephen Peterson

How does anthropogenic disturbance — vessel traffic, ecotourism, and noise pollution — affect the behaviour and cohesion of beluga whale pods during their annual summer migration to Hudson Bay, and what are the implications for three critically endangered populations?

My Role

Independent research project within the Arctic Field Biology Course. I designed the study protocol, conducted all data collection, processed and quality-controlled field datasets in R, and presented findings through seminar presentations and infographic reports to academic and public audiences. The project addresses three critically endangered populations: the St. Lawrence Estuary Beluga (fewer than 900 individuals), the Southern Resident Killer Whale (approximately 75 individuals), and globally threatened coral species implicated in food web research.

Collaborators & Institutional Partners

Beluga Bits citizen science initiative · Assiniboine Park Zoo · Polar Bear International · University of Toronto Mississauga · University of Manitoba

Methods

Data collection integrated three complementary approaches: boat-based transect surveys to document pod composition, behaviour states, and proximity to vessel traffic; systematic acoustic monitoring to record vocalisation rates and acoustic complexity under varying disturbance conditions; and remote live-camera footage analysis for continuous behavioural observation independent of survey vessel presence. Large field datasets were processed and quality-controlled in R, with anomalies flagged and documented. The Canadian Boating Licence was required for vessel operation.
