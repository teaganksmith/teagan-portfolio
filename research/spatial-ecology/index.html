Global Coral Reef Health & the Carbon Footprint of Marine Conservation Science

Darling Lab, Department of Ecology & Evolutionary Biology, University of Toronto · Toronto, ON · September 2021 – April 2022 · Advisor: Dr. Emily Darling

What are the patterns of geographic bias in who conducts marine conservation science, and what are the carbon costs of the field-intensive research model — and what are the implications for equitable, sustainable conservation practice?

My Role

Research project in the Darling Lab at the University of Toronto under Dr. Emily Darling. I conducted spatial data analysis and visualization of global coral reef health indicators using the MERMAID marine spatial platform and R, processing and interpreting multi-variable ecological datasets to support peer-reviewed research. I also assessed the carbon footprint and geographic bias of marine conservation science, applying policy-relevant biogeographic analysis across global reef systems. Responsibilities included systematic literature searches using PubMed and academic databases, and maintaining accurate data records throughout the project lifecycle.

Methods

Analysis was conducted using MERMAID for coral reef ecological data aggregation and R (ggplot2, tidyverse) for statistical modelling and data visualization. Biogeographic patterns were assessed by mapping publication origin, research institution affiliation, and field site location against reef system distribution using QGIS. Carbon footprint analysis applied established emissions accounting methodologies to researcher travel patterns extracted from the literature. Systematic literature searches were conducted using PubMed and Web of Science.
