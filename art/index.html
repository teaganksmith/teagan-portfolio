Art

I make oil paintings and biological illustrations that grow out of the same questions my research asks: about the sublime, about ecological relationship and loss, and about what it means to pay close attention to the living world at a moment when so much of it is disappearing.

The oil paintings are concerned with climate grief. I am interested in the macabre register of the sublime, the awe and fear of nature as something far more powerful than us, especially now, as worsening disasters make that power impossible to ignore. The biological illustrations work differently, attending to form, texture, and the logic of an organism's structure. Paying close attention to nature's intricate beauty and design.

I want this work to make you feel something you have been avoiding. Most of us grew up knowing the world was changing, felt the pull to help, and then watched adulthood quietly absorb that impulse into obligation and distance. I think looking directly at it, holding both the grief and the possibility of doing something, is exactly the antidote to the apathy that follows fear.
