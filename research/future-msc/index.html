Evolutionary Genomics: C4 Photosynthesis Assembly & Cetacean Protein Evolution

Sage Lab, Department of Ecology & Evolutionary Biology, University of Toronto · Toronto, ON · May – September 2020 · Advisor: Dr. Tammy Sage · NSERC Undergraduate Student Research Award recipient

What are the evolutionary genomic mechanisms underlying the convergent assembly of C4 leaf structure — and what does selection on cetacean cytokine receptors reveal about the rate of protein evolution in aquatic mammals?

My Role & Funding

This position was funded by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) Undergraduate Student Research Award — nationally competitive federal research funding from Canada's primary science funding agency, awarded on academic merit to support undergraduate research. I was based in the Sage Lab at the University of Toronto, conducting evolutionary genomics research in collaboration with the International C4 Rice Consortium. Responsibilities included evolutionary genomics analysis using PAML, systematic literature review, maintaining research records, and supporting experimental methodology development.

Selected Research Paper

"Evidence of purifying selection in cytokine receptor 2 (CXCR2) reveals differences in cetacean rate of protein evolution"

EEB460 — University of Toronto · 2020

GWAS data quality control, PAML molecular evolution analysis, integration of genetic and proteomic data types. Examined selection pressures on cetacean immune receptors as a window into the molecular adaptations accompanying the return to aquatic environments.

Methods

Analysis used PAML (Phylogenetic Analysis by Maximum Likelihood) for molecular evolution modelling, with custom bioinformatics pipelines for sequence alignment and clade model comparisons. GWAS data quality control pipelines were applied to filter and process genomic datasets. Proteomic data were integrated with genetic data to identify signatures of purifying and positive selection across cetacean lineages. Systematic literature review supported experimental methodology and provided comparative evolutionary context.

Key Findings

Evidence of purifying selection on CXCR2 in cetaceans suggests strong functional constraint on this immune receptor, consistent with the hypothesis that aquatic adaptation involved reorganisation of immune function rather than relaxed selection. Differences in evolutionary rate between cetacean lineages reflect divergent ecological pressures across the Odontoceti–Mysticeti split.

Note: This was an undergraduate research paper. Full text available upon request.
