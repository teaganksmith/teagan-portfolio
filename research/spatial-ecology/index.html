The Biogeography and Carbon Footprint of Coral Reef Science

EEB397, University of Toronto · Toronto, ON · November 2020 – April 2021 · Supervisor: Emily Darling

What is the carbon footprint of coral reef science, and how is it distributed across biogeographic regions of coral reef diversity?

My Role

I filtered and restructured a coral reef publications dataset, isolating field-based studies with first author affiliation data. I then conducted spatial and carbon footprint analyses in R Studio.

Methods

Reef health data was pulled from MERMAID (Marine Ecological Research Management Aid) and integrated with a coral reef publications dataset in R Studio. Using the tidyverse, I calculated total unique travel routes, number of countries represented, total and mean distance traveled per paper, and estimated carbon emissions (tCO2e). Geographic patterns were visualised using ggplot2, linking publication records to field site locations and researcher institutional affiliations across biogeographic regions, cross-referenced against MERMAID reef health indicators.
