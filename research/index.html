Evidence of Purifying Selection in Cytokine Receptor 2 (CXCR2) Reveals Differences in Cetacean Rate of Protein Evolution

Independent analysis of CXCR2 protein evolution across 20 cetacean and artiodactyl species, assessing whether purifying selection on this immune receptor contributes to reduced cancer incidence in large-bodied baleen whales. EEB460, University of Toronto, December 2023.

Quantifying the Frequency and Distribution of Beluga Whale Scars Through Photo Identification in the Churchill River

Independent field research assessing anthropogenic injury to the Churchill River beluga subpopulation. Conducted ten boat surveys, photographed surfacing pods, and analysed 5,642 photos from a 10,239-image catalogue. Conducted with Beluga Bits, Assiniboine Park Zoo, and Polar Bear International. Churchill, MB, July–August 2023.

NSERC Undergraduate Student Research Award-funded independent literature review and research on C4 photosynthesis, covering convergent evolution, Kranz anatomy, gene expression, and the significance of C4 crop research. Sage Lab, University of Toronto, May–September 2020.

Methods & tools: I work with bioacoustic analysis software (Raven Lite, Audacity, R/seewave), QGIS for spatial analysis and mapping, and statistical analysis in R (ggplot2, tidyverse). I have experience with PAML for molecular evolution, phylogenetic reconstruction in MEGA and Mr. Bayes, sequence alignment in ClustalO, and tree visualization in FigTree. I work with NCBI and Ensembl for sequence acquisition and BLAST search, Python for large-scale biodiversity data analysis including multi-million record GBIF datasets, and Google Earth Engine for remote sensing and geospatial analysis. My field methods include boat-based transect surveys, photo identification, and systematic classification. I produce scientific illustrations as a working part of my research and write about science for public audiences.
